Ten years. 620 books. Here are the 50 that stuck.

By Sean Patrick Phelps November 2026 13 min read

When I finished grad school, I made myself a promise: the learning doesn’t stop just because the classes do.

The rule was one nonfiction book a week. Not a goal for the good weeks. A standard for every week, including the summer seasons, the travel weeks, and the ones with no room on the calendar.

The first book was How to Win Friends & Influence People, in January 2017. Ten years later, the count is 620. I finished this year’s 52 early, so the rest of 2026 is bonus reading.

I keep every one in a spreadsheet: the week, the title, the author, and a yes when it’s done. A lot of them came through my headphones on a ruck, with a weighted pack on my back. At the end of each year, I bold the five that changed something. How I lead, how I parent, how I think about money, risk, or what a good life looks like.

This post is those bolded books. Five a year, ten years, 50 books. For each one, here’s why it made the cut.

One bookA top-five pick

Every spine is a book I finished. Tap a year to jump to its picks.

It’s the same math as 52 Trash Bags, my challenge to pick up one bag of trash a week for a year. One bag doesn’t clean a city, and one book won’t make you smarter. Fifty-two a year, for ten years, changes how you think. Small, consistent action, compounded, is the most underrated advantage there is.

One book won’t change your life. One a week, for ten years, will change how you think.

2017 59 books read. See all 59

01Zero to One

Peter Thiel

Thiel asks a question I still use to pressure-test any idea: what important truth do very few people agree with you on? Competition is for copycats. The goal is to build something so different it creates its own category. Stop asking how to win the market you see and start asking what market nobody has built yet.

02Fooled by Randomness

Nassim Nicholas Taleb

The book that taught me to respect luck without worshipping it. Taleb shows how often we mistake a lucky streak for skill, in markets and in life. The lesson for any leader: judge a decision by the process, not the outcome, and stay humble when the dice land your way.

03Eleven Rings: The Soul of Success

Phil Jackson

Eleven championships, and Jackson credits most of them to getting stars to play for something bigger than themselves. The triangle offense was the system. Trust was the real work. It’s the best book I know on coaching talent that doesn’t think it needs a coach, which is exactly when coaching matters most.

04Where Are the Customers’ Yachts?

Fred Schwed

Written in 1940 and still the funniest book about Wall Street. The title comes from a visitor admiring the bankers’ boats in the harbor, then asking where the customers’ boats were. Schwed’s point hits hard: follow the incentives, and be careful with anyone who gets paid whether you win or lose.

05A Man for All Markets

Edward Thorp

Thorp beat blackjack with math, then beat Wall Street with the same discipline. What stuck with me was the temperament more than the formulas. Measure the odds, size every bet so one loss can’t wipe you out, and keep enough edge to play another day.

2018 57 books read. See all 57

06The Case Against Sugar

Gary Taubes

A book that makes you look at your plate differently. Taubes builds the case that sugar, not just calories, sits behind a lot of modern disease. Whether or not you buy every argument, the habit it builds is worth having: question the default, especially when the default is printed on a food label.

07I Don’t Have Enough Faith to Be an Atheist

Norman Geisler & Frank Turek

A book that treats the biggest questions with the seriousness they deserve. Geisler and Turek lay out a step-by-step case, from logic to history, for why they believe what they believe. You don’t have to land where they land to value the method. Know what you believe, and know why.

08Thinking in Bets

Annie Duke

A champion poker player on making decisions without all the information, which describes every decision that matters. Duke separates the quality of a choice from the quality of its outcome. It sharpened the poker frame for me: go all in when you’ve honestly assessed your hand, then learn from the result either way.

09Life 3.0

Max Tegmark

I read it in 2018, years before AI showed up in every inbox, and it reads even better now. Tegmark lays out the futures intelligent machines could create, good and bad, and argues the outcome depends on choices people make early. The people who shape a technology are the ones who engage with it before it’s obvious.

10To Sell Is Human

Daniel Pink

Pink’s argument is that we’re all in sales now, whether we’re pitching a customer, a boss, or our own kids at bedtime. His ABCs (attunement, buoyancy, and clarity) replace the old always-be-closing playbook. It’s the book I’d hand anyone who thinks selling is something you do to people instead of for them.

2019 54 books read. See all 54

11Your Money or Your Life

Vicki Robin

One question at the center of this book changes how you see a paycheck: how many hours of your life did that purchase cost? Robin reframes money as life energy, and suddenly every spending decision gets clearer. Enough is a number you can define, and defining it is a kind of freedom.

12Trillion Dollar Coach

Eric Schmidt, Jonathan Rosenberg, and Alan Eagle

Bill Campbell coached Steve Jobs, Larry Page, and a generation of Silicon Valley leaders, and he never stopped being a football coach. His secret was simple and rare: care about your people as people first. This is coach ’em up in book form, and it belongs on every manager’s shelf.

13Extreme Ownership

Jocko Willink

Two Navy SEALs with one rule: there are no bad teams, only bad leaders. When something goes wrong, the leader owns it. All of it. It pairs perfectly with The Loop. Own the miss, measure it, adjust, and go again.

14What You Do Is Who You Are

Ben Horowitz

The title is a line I live by, and the book proves it holds up. Horowitz studies culture through Toussaint Louverture, the samurai, and Genghis Khan, and lands on one truth. Culture isn’t what you say. It’s what your people do when no one is checking.

15The Man Who Solved the Market

Gregory Zuckerman

Jim Simons was a codebreaker and mathematician who built the most successful hedge fund in history by trusting data over gut feel. The lesson isn’t to go build an algorithm. It’s that a small edge, applied with discipline over and over, beats brilliance applied once.

2020 91 books read. See all 91

16Never Split the Difference

Chris Voss

An FBI hostage negotiator on why logic loses to empathy. Label the emotion, mirror the words, and ask calibrated questions that start with how and what. I’ve never read a sales book that teaches more about listening, and every tactic is usable the same day you read it.

17The 5 Second Rule

Mel Robbins

The simplest tool on this whole list. When you feel the urge to act, count 5-4-3-2-1 and move before your brain talks you out of it. It works because hesitation is the enemy, not a lack of ability. Start before you feel ready, five seconds at a time.

18It Takes What It Takes

Trevor Moawad

Moawad worked with Russell Wilson and championed neutral thinking: not positive, not negative, just the next right action. Positivity can be as fragile as negativity. Neutral holds up under pressure. It’s the mindset you want when a season gets hard.

19Dad Is Fat

Jim Gaffigan

A comedian raising five kids in a two-bedroom Manhattan apartment. It earns its spot because a list of 50 serious books needs one that reminds you parenting is chaos, and the chaos is the good part. Proof you can have it all, as long as you can laugh at it.

20The Courage to Be Happy

Ichiro Kishimi & Fumitake Koga

The sequel to The Courage to Be Disliked, and the one that hit harder. Through Adlerian psychology, it argues that happiness comes from contribution, and that respect can’t be demanded, only offered. A powerful book for anyone who leads, teaches, or parents.

2021 59 books read. See all 59

21Nonviolent Communication

Marshall Rosenberg

Rosenberg gives you a four-part structure for hard conversations: observations, feelings, needs, and requests. It sounds soft. It isn’t. It’s how you turn me against you into us vs. the goal, and it’s the most practical communication framework I know.

22The Forgetting Machine

Rodrigo Quian Quiroga

A neuroscientist on why forgetting is a feature, not a bug. Your brain discards details so it can find patterns and think in concepts. It reframed this whole project for me. Fifty-two books a year isn’t about remembering everything. It’s about keeping the ideas that change how you act.

23Breathe

Rickson Gracie & Peter Maguire

The memoir of jiu-jitsu’s most feared fighter, and a book about control under pressure. Gracie’s edge was breath, calm, and connection while everyone else panicked. The best competitors don’t avoid discomfort. They get comfortable inside it.

24No Shortcuts to the Top

Ed Viesturs

Ed Viesturs climbed all fourteen of the world’s 8,000-meter peaks without supplemental oxygen, and he grew up in Rockford, Illinois, same as me. His rule belongs on every big goal: getting to the top is optional, getting down is mandatory. Ambition and judgment have to travel together.

25Into Thin Air

Jon Krakauer

The 1996 Everest disaster, told by a climber who was there. It’s a mountaineering classic and a leadership case study: summit fever, ignored deadlines, and teams that stopped acting like teams when the weather turned. Read it before you set any goal without a turnaround time.

2022 79 books read. See all 79

26438 Days

Jonathan Franklin

A fisherman from El Salvador drifted across the Pacific for 438 days and survived on raw fish, turtles, and rainwater. What kept him alive was a reason to keep going and a mind that refused to quit. It’s the most extreme proof I’ve read that you should never underestimate what a person can endure.

27Hero on a Mission

Donald Miller

Miller argues every person plays one of four roles at any moment: victim, villain, hero, or guide. Then he gives you a plan to live as the hero and grow into the guide who helps others win. Write your eulogy, set your vision, and review it daily. Simple, and it works.

28greatness

David Cook

A short book from the longtime mental coach of the San Antonio Spurs, built on one idea he calls selfless exceptionalism. Greatness isn’t winning for yourself. It’s being exceptional for the people around you. About a hundred pages, and worth a reread every season.

29Solito

Javier Zamora

A memoir of a nine-year-old making the journey from El Salvador to the United States, carried by strangers who became family. Heartbreaking and full of grace, it’s a reminder that young people are capable of far more than we expect of them, and that kindness from a stranger can change a life.

30Nasty, Brutish, and Short

Scott Hershovitz

A philosopher and law professor learns philosophy from his own kids’ questions about fairness, punishment, and rights. It’s funny, smart, and the best case I’ve seen for taking young minds seriously. Kids are natural philosophers. Most adults just stop asking.

2023 58 books read. See all 58

31Let My People Go Surfing

Yvon Chouinard

The founder of Patagonia on building a company that stands for something. Chouinard built his business around quality, the outdoors, and doing less harm, and proved a values-first company can still compete and win. Patagonia is one of the brands I measure my own work against, and this is why.

32Tears in the Darkness

Michael Norman & Elizabeth Norman

The Bataan Death March, told from the American, Filipino, and Japanese sides. It’s brutal and essential, and a hard reset on what counts as a bad day, and a reminder of how much weight ordinary people can carry when they have to.

33A Mile at a Time

Mark Macy, Travis Macy & Patrick Regan

Mark Macy, a pioneer of adventure racing, faces an early-onset Alzheimer’s diagnosis by doing what he knows: competing, with his son Travis beside him. It’s a father-and-son story about meeting the hardest news with action. You can’t control the diagnosis. You can control the next mile.

34Unreasonable Hospitality

Will Guidara

Guidara helped take Eleven Madison Park to number one in the world by giving guests more than they ever expected, like serving a street-cart hot dog as a fine-dining course. The lesson travels far beyond restaurants: the extra five percent of care is where loyalty lives.

35The Art of Power

Thich Nhat Hanh

A Zen master’s take on power. Real power, he argues, is happiness, freedom, and the ability to stay steady while everyone around you chases more. For a competitive person, this book is medicine. You can want to win and still be at peace.

2024 52 books read. See all 52

36The New Confessions of an Economic Hit Man

John Perkins

Perkins tells his story as a consultant who helped load developing nations with debt they couldn’t repay. Read it as one man’s account and decide for yourself. The lesson travels either way: incentives explain behavior, and the person writing the forecast often profits from it.

37The Expectation Effect

David Robson

Robson shows how expectations shape health, performance, and even aging. Beliefs aren’t fluff. They change outcomes. It’s the science underneath don’t underestimate: what you expect from a team, or from yourself, is often what you get.

38The Ruthless Elimination of Hurry

John Mark Comer

Hurry is the enemy of a good life, and Comer offers a practical plan to slow down through silence, Sabbath, simplicity, and slowing. For anyone who loves a full calendar, it’s a needed correction. You can have it all. Just not all at once, and not in a rush.

39The Misbehavior of Markets

Benoit Mandelbrot & Richard Hudson

The father of fractal geometry on why markets are far wilder than the models assume. Extreme events aren’t rare flukes. They’re built into the system. Plan for the storm, not the average day, whether you’re managing a portfolio, a business, or a summer season.

40Be Useful

Arnold Schwarzenegger

Seven tools for life from a man who went from an Austrian village to Mr. Olympia, Hollywood, and the governor’s office. Have a clear vision. Never think small. Outwork everyone. Sell your ideas. The title is the whole philosophy, and it’s a good one.

2025 52 books read. See all 52

41Never Enough

Andrew Wilkinson

The founder of Tiny on building a fortune and discovering that more never fills the hole. It’s candid about ambition, anxiety, and the trap of always chasing the next win. A gut check for every builder: define enough before the scoreboard defines it for you.

42Scarcity

Sendhil Mullainathan & Eldar Shafir

Scarcity of time, money, or anything else tunnels your vision and taxes your mental bandwidth. Busy people make worse decisions for the same reason broke people do. Build slack into the system and you think more clearly. Every leader should design for this.

43The Art of Winning

Bill Belichick

Six Super Bowl rings as a head coach, and the lessons come down to preparation, accountability, and doing your job. There’s a Latin motto I keep in reserve, Amat Victoria Curam: victory loves preparation. Belichick is that motto in human form.

44Economical Writing

Deirdre Nansen McCloskey

A short book of rules for clear writing from an economist who can’t stand jargon. Cut the throat-clearing, use plain words, and make every sentence earn its place. It’s the rulebook I want Field Notes to follow, and it will make your emails better by Friday.

45Die With Zero

Bill Perkins

Perkins argues the goal isn’t to maximize money. It’s to maximize experiences, and many of them have an expiration date. You can’t hike Half Dome at 85 the way you can at 45. Plan your memory dividends on purpose, while you can still collect them.

2026 59 books read. See all 59

46The Choice

Dr. Edith Eva Eger

Eger survived Auschwitz as a teenager and became a psychologist who helps others heal. Her message: you can’t choose what happens to you, but you can choose how you respond. If she could choose freedom after that, the rest of us are out of excuses.

47Just Keep Buying

Nick Maggiulli

The most practical personal finance book I’ve read, and the title is the strategy. Save consistently, invest consistently, and let time do the heavy lifting. It’s 52 Trash Bags for your money: small, boring, repeated actions that compound into something big.

48How to Speak Whale

Tom Mustill

A filmmaker nearly crushed by a breaching humpback goes looking for how AI might help us understand animal communication. It sits at a wild intersection of nature and technology, and a reminder that the most exciting uses of AI may be ones nobody has imagined yet.

49The Infinity Machine

Sebastian Mallaby

The story of Demis Hassabis and DeepMind, from chess prodigy to Nobel Prize to the race toward superintelligence. It’s a portrait of a founder who chose a mission over money and kept score in scientific breakthroughs. Essential reading for anyone building with AI.

50The Steps

Sylvester Stallone

Before Rocky, Stallone was broke, typecast, and turned down by nearly everyone. He wrote the script himself and refused to sell it unless he could star in it. The steps in Philadelphia stand for every time he failed to reach the next level and climbed anyway. An underdog story from the original underdog.
