100 Experiences That Set You Apart

By Sean Patrick Phelps October 2026 24 min read

In the summer of 1994, a 21-year-old at Queen’s University was operating a residential painting franchise. He recruited the crews, sold the jobs, and managed production.

When the season ended, he sold his stake in the franchise, pooled the cash with his older brother, bought a used BMW, and drove across the country. Soon after, the two of them started Zip2. In 1999, Compaq bought it for more than $300 million.

The student was Kimbal Musk. The franchise was College Pro Painters.

Nobody handed him a career. He had a rare skill, the ability to build a team from nothing and make it produce, and he pointed it at the next thing.

If you just finished a season, you have that skill too. This list is 100 places to point it.

How this is ranked

Every item is scored on five things: how much real responsibility you hold, how few people your age have done it, how good the network is, whether the skill still pays in twenty years, and whether you can get in.

That last one matters. A Rhodes Scholarship sets you apart more than almost anything on earth, and roughly nobody gets one, so it does not outrank things you can go do this fall.

Each entry carries a tag so you can scan for the kind of bet you want to make:

  • Operate lead people and outcomes
  • Own equity in something real
  • Capital get funded to build
  • Fellowship structured programs with a cohort
  • Public government, politics, and service
  • Global outside the United States
  • Frontier AI and deep tech
  • Craft durable skills
  • Network access to people
  • School degrees worth having
  • Wild hard, physical, remote

You can’t go after all 100. Pick three.

01College Works District Manager

This is number one, and it is not close for the specific year in front of you.

Here is the argument. As a District Manager you recruit and hire your own team, train them, sell alongside them, manage production and quality, handle payroll and customer escalations, and carry a real revenue number. You lead 40 to 50 people. You do it while you are still enrolled in college.

Now compare that to the alternatives at the same age.

A consulting analyst at a top firm has zero direct reports and does not touch a customer. An investment banking analyst has zero direct reports and does not touch a customer. A software engineer at a great company owns a feature. A marketing coordinator owns a calendar. A Teach for America corps member owns a classroom, which is real and hard, and it is one room.

Almost every prestigious entry-level job in America gives a 21-year-old a narrow slice of someone else’s business and calls it experience.

You get the whole business. Hiring, firing, pricing, delivery, cash, and reputation. You find out what you are made of in June when three people quit and it rains for a week.

That is the rarest thing on this list, and it is the one you are already qualified for.

One honest caveat: it may not be the industry you want to stay in forever, which is the reason the other 99 exist. Do the DM year. Then move up, or go do one of the next 99.

The rest of the ranking

Filter by tag to find your kind of bet, or read straight through. The order is the ranking.

Showing all 99

02Come back and grow your branchOperate

Multi-crew leadership means you manage crew leaders, which is a genuinely different job from managing one crew. Very few people learn it before 30. Almost nobody learns it before 25.

03Become chief of staff to a founder or CEOOperate

The highest-slope job available to someone in their twenties. You sit in every decision, meet every investor, and absorb pattern recognition that usually takes fifteen years to accumulate. Small companies hire for this more often than people realize, and they hire for judgment rather than pedigree.

04Buy a small business and operate itOwn

Home services, cleaning, landscaping, pest control, or a contractor. Thousands of owners in their sixties have no succession plan and no buyer. You have already recruited crews and managed production, which makes you more qualified than most MBAs to operate a company doing $500,000 to $2 million. Ownership beats employment on every axis except comfort.

05Join Antler as a founder in residenceCapital

Antler invests at day zero, before you have a company, a co-founder, or even an idea. Residencies last five to ten weeks across more than 30 global locations, and teams that pass the investment committee typically receive $250,000 to $500,000. Critically for you, they explicitly recruit people with sales and domain backgrounds, not just engineers. Acceptance sits around 3 percent. Applications are reviewed year-round.

06Become a Claude Corps fellowFellowship

Anthropic’s fellowship places early-career people inside nonprofits for twelve months to build AI tools and systems, at $85,000 plus full benefits, with intensive training and a national cohort. The eligibility bar is deliberately wide: 18 or older, U.S. work authorization, under two years of full-time experience, no degree required. Cohorts start January 2027 and August 2027 with rolling recruitment. The application asks about a time you made an impact and a time you learned from a setback, which is exactly what a painting season produces.

07Get into Y CombinatorCapital

Three months, a check, and the strongest founder network on the planet. They fund very young founders every batch. The application is short, and the deadline comes several times a year.

08Win a Thiel FellowshipCapital

Two years and $250,000, with no equity taken, for anyone 22 or younger without a university degree. You must leave school to accept. Roughly 20 to 30 fellows are selected annually from thousands of applicants, and applications are accepted on a rolling basis. Long odds, free to enter, and the act of writing the application forces a clarity most people never reach.

09Take a Schwarzman ScholarshipSchool

A fully funded one-year master’s in global affairs at Tsinghua University in Beijing, 150 scholars a year from around the world, no Chinese language required, ages 18 to 28. Applications typically close in early September, about a year before the program starts. Modeled on Rhodes and Marshall, and far younger, which means the alumni network is still small enough to be personal.

10Be the first or second go-to-market hire at a Series A or B AI companyFrontier

AI absorbed roughly a third of all venture funding in 2026 and the capital concentrated hard into category leaders. Skip the giant research labs, which hire a handful of senior PhDs. Target companies that raised $100 million to $500 million and are visibly hiring. Big enough to have built something, small enough that you own the number.

11Join EO AcceleratorOwn

The Entrepreneurs’ Organization operates it for owners and founders doing $250,000 to $999,999 a year, in more than 90 cities. Four full-day learning events, monthly accountability groups, $1,750 for the year. Cross a million and you graduate into full EO membership. If you own your business, this is the fastest route into a room full of people leading seven and eight-figure companies. You get there in your twenties instead of your forties.

12Become a Coro Fellow in Public AffairsFellowship

Nine months, about 64 fellows chosen nationally, rotating through placements in business, government, labor, and nonprofit sectors across Los Angeles, New York, Pittsburgh, San Francisco, and St. Louis. Selection is a full-day immersive assessment, not an interview. Eighty years of alumni. Applications close in mid-January.

13Join the Govern For America fellowshipPublic

Two years, paid, in real state and local government roles. Placements include Missouri, Colorado, Michigan, Louisiana, Texas, and Pennsylvania. Applications for the 2027 cohort post this fall.

14Win a Fulbright English Teaching AssistantshipGlobal

The most portable credential a new graduate can get. Every employer and admissions committee in the country knows the name. The national deadline lands in early October, campus deadlines come weeks earlier, and any U.S. citizen holding a bachelor’s by the start date is eligible. Find your campus Fulbright adviser in the spring, not the fall.

15Take a Congress-Bundestag Youth Exchange fellowshipGlobal

The best program almost nobody applies to. The U.S. State Department and the German Bundestag send 65 to 75 Americans aged 18 to 24 to Germany for a year: two months of intensive language training, a semester of coursework in your field, and a three-to-five-month internship with a German company. Airfare, tuition, housing, insurance, and a monthly stipend are covered. No German required. It prioritizes business, technical, vocational, and agricultural backgrounds, which is to say people like you. Deadlines fall around November 1.

16Work a political campaign, and ask for the organizer jobPublic

There is no faster environment in America. Field organizing is recruiting, training, and daily production management under a hard deadline with a binary public outcome. It’s your season with a different scoreboard, compressed into four months. Don’t take the internship. You can lead volunteers and hit contact targets, and campaign managers are always short on people who can.

17Get a U.S. Digital Corps fellowshipPublic

Two years inside the federal government for early-career technologists across software engineering, data, product, design, and cybersecurity. Salary, benefits, and a built-in path to convert to a permanent role. Fellows have served at more than two dozen agencies. The next cohort’s applications open later this year.

18Join Teach for AmericaPublic

Two years in a high-need classroom. Applications for the 2027 corps open August 2026, with deadlines through March and first-come regional placement, so apply early if you want a specific city. One thing to check: federal AmeriCorps funding was disrupted in 2025 and remains contested. Confirm the current education award with your regional office rather than a webpage.

19Win an Emergent Ventures grantCapital

Led by economist Tyler Cowen at the Mercatus Center. It funds ambitious “zero to one” ideas from anyone 13 or older, awards commonly range from $10,000 to $100,000, and decisions arrive in weeks. The application takes under thirty minutes and asks nothing about your credentials or your business plan. It’s the best return on half an hour available to a young person in America.

20Take over a family businessOwn

If one exists in your family, stop treating it as the fallback. It’s a company with revenue, customers, and a brand, being handed to someone who knows how to lead people.

21Join South Park Commons as a founder fellowCapital

Built for the pre-idea moment. You don’t need traction, a team, or a concept. Fellows receive $1 million, structured as $400,000 upfront plus $600,000 committed to your next round, for 7 percent. Bootcamps are held twice a year out of San Francisco, New York, and Bangalore. It skews technical, so bring a builder.

22Campaign for local officePublic

School board, city council, or a county seat. Most races at this level are decided by whoever knocks on the most doors, which is the one thing you’re already elite at. Win or lose, you’ll never be intimidated by a room again.

23Become a general manager in training with a written path to buyOwn

The apprenticeship version of acquisition. You operate the business for two years under an agreement that transfers ownership on a defined schedule. Owners without a succession plan are far more open to this conversation than young people assume.

24Get an Anthropic Fellows placementFrontier

Four months of full-time AI safety research with mentorship from Anthropic researchers, a weekly stipend around $3,850, and a large compute budget. No PhD, no prior ML experience, and no published papers required, though you do need to code well in Python. More than 40 percent of the first cohort went on to work at Anthropic full-time.

25Get into MATSFrontier

Ten to twelve weeks of funded AI alignment, security, and governance research in Berkeley and London, with mentors from Anthropic, Google DeepMind, OpenAI, and Redwood. Around $5,000 a month plus compute, housing, and meals, with a funded six-to-twelve-month extension that most fellows receive. Tracks now include policy and field-building, so it isn’t only for engineers.

26Compete in the Global Student Entrepreneur AwardsNetwork

The Entrepreneurs’ Organization operates the premier global competition for students who own and operate a business while enrolled. Prize pool over $100,000, and local chapter qualifiers feed national and global finals. Judging weighs your entrepreneurial journey, meaning the setbacks and what you learned, not a polished idea.

27Organize 1 Million Cups, or start a chapter where none existsNetwork

The Kauffman Foundation launched 1MC in 2012 and it now operates in well over 100 communities, staffed by volunteer organizers who call themselves Caffeinators. You spend one morning a week putting local founders on stage. Inside a year you’ll know every entrepreneur in your market. Founding a chapter is better still, because it makes you the ecosystem in a town that doesn’t have one, and Kauffman hands you the playbook.

28Get to a One Young World SummitNetwork

More than 2,200 delegates from over 190 countries, ages 18 to 35, and every delegate becomes an Ambassador inside a network of 20,500. Cape Town in November 2026, Tokyo in 2027. There are three doors. The Leading Scholarship covers travel, hotel, and meals, and closes October 31, 2026. A corporate-sponsored scholarship is the second. The third is your employer sending you as part of its delegation. Most people only ever find the first door.

29Join the Global Shapers CommunityNetwork

The World Economic Forum’s under-30 network, with more than 10,000 members across roughly 450 city hubs in about 150 countries. Applications are rolling and go through your local hub rather than through Geneva, which is the part most people miss. Shapers do reach Davos and the Sustainable Development Impact Meetings in New York, but only a fraction each year. Treat the hub as the work and the Forum as the ceiling.

30Do a season on the Antarctic Support ContractWild

Leidos is the prime contractor for the National Science Foundation’s United States Antarctic Program, staffing McMurdo, South Pole, and Palmer stations. Austral summer contracts last from October to February, and entry-level roles exist in logistics, supply, dispatch, and food service. You’ll be one of a few thousand Americans alive who has spent a season on the ice, and every interviewer for the rest of your life will ask about it.

31Get into the Recurse CenterCraft

A free, self-directed programming retreat in Brooklyn. Six or twelve weeks, new batches every six weeks, remote option available. No curriculum and no teachers. You need to already write code well enough to know you like it. More than 3,000 alumni, a motto of “Never Graduate,” and a business model funded by post grad recruiting rather than tuition.

32Become an early employee at a company crossing 50 peopleOperate

That threshold is the moment a business either builds real process or breaks. Someone who understands recruiting, training, and production management becomes indispensable overnight.

33Win a Rhodes, Marshall, Mitchell, or Gates Cambridge ScholarshipSchool

Fully funded graduate study in the United Kingdom or Ireland, and a permanent membership card to a global elite. Campus nomination deadlines land in August and September, two months before the national deadlines, so the real deadline is earlier than you think. Very long odds. Apply anyway.

34Become a Knight-Hennessy ScholarSchool

Full funding for any graduate degree at Stanford plus a leadership development program on top. October deadline. Long odds, and worth a weekend.

35Take a Princeton in Asia or Princeton in Africa fellowshipGlobal

Year-long professional placements across education, public health, economic development, and communications. Open to graduates of any university, not just Princeton, which surprises most applicants.

36Become a Luce ScholarGlobal

A year in Asia with a professional placement matched to your field, built for people with no prior Asia experience. It requires campus nomination, which means far fewer competitors than the prestige suggests.

37Join the Peace CorpsGlobal

Two years. Still the clearest signal of durability a 23-year-old can send, and it comes with noncompetitive federal hiring eligibility afterward.

38Enter the JET ProgramGlobal

Assistant language teacher or coordinator of international relations placements across Japan, open to graduates from more than 55 countries, renewable up to four times. Applications open in the fall.

39Win a Watson FellowshipGlobal

A year of independent international travel chasing your own question, awarded through partner colleges. If your school participates, the applicant pool is often small enough that a strong candidate has real odds. Check whether you’re eligible before you assume you aren’t.

40Sell a business you builtOwn

Even a small one. Nothing on a resume does what “founded, operated, and exited” does, and the number doesn’t have to be large for the story to be true.

41Buy a single-unit franchiseOwn

A business with the operating manual already written, for someone who already understands unit economics, territory, and labor. Lower risk than a startup, and far more instructive than a job.

42Get a 1517 Fund Medici GrantCapital

The 1517 team came out of the Thiel Foundation and now backs dropouts, students, and builders without degrees. Medici grants are $1,000 in fast, non-dilutive cash for young people who prove they can ship, and the fund invests from $50,000 up to seven figures in the ones that grow. Their early bets include the founders of Figma and Loom. Small check, serious network.

43Get a Z Fellows spotCapital

$10,000 and a short, intense in-person sprint in San Francisco with founders and investors. You don’t have to quit school or your job, no company incorporation required, and solo applicants are welcome.

44Work a season on a wildland fire crewWild

Federal and state agencies hire seasonal firefighters every spring, no prior experience required beyond a fitness test and training. Physically brutal, well paid with overtime and hazard pay, and it builds a composure that transfers to every high-pressure room you’ll ever be in.

45Fish a season in AlaskaWild

Commercial salmon and crab crews hire greenhorns each summer. Long hours, real danger, and a shot at clearing serious money in a few months. It’s also the fastest cure for believing a hard week at a desk is a hard week.

46Take a Bill Emerson National Hunger FellowshipPublic

Five months embedded in a community organization, then six months in Washington working federal anti-hunger policy. Field to policy in a single year, a structure almost no other program offers.

47Become a Dorm Room Fund investment partnerNetwork

A student-led venture firm that spun out of First Round Capital, backed by limited partners including Marc Andreessen and Chris Dixon, investing in student-founded companies. You can do it alongside a full course load. The alumni network of student investment partners is the real product.

48Scout for a venture fundNetwork

Plenty of funds pay young operators to source deals. You get paid to meet founders, which is the best networking arbitrage in business.

49Work directly for a second-time founderOperate

Someone who has already built and sold a company will teach you more in two years than a first-timer will in five. They’ve made the expensive mistakes and they’ll tell you about them.

50Do a Techstars acceleratorCapital

More cities, more cohorts, and better odds than Y Combinator, with a mentor network that earns its keep for a first-time founder.

51Join gener8torCapital

Strong Midwest presence, including Kansas City. If you want to build where you already live and know people, this is the on-ramp.

52Qualify as a Leader of Tomorrow at the St. Gallen SymposiumGlobal

Two hundred young people a year join 600 senior leaders in Switzerland for three days of cross-generational debate. Travel, board, and lodging covered, 10,000 alumni. The famous route is the Global Essay Competition, which requires graduate enrollment and closes February 1. The route almost nobody knows about is the Global Leadership Challenge, open to bachelor’s students, where five of the 100 participants earn a place at the symposium.

53Take a working holiday visa to Australia or New ZealandWild

Both countries issue visas letting Americans under 31 live and work for a year. People do farm work, hospitality, and trades, then travel on what they earn. Almost nobody from your graduating class will do it, and it turns one year into a story that lasts a career.

54Work a season on a custom harvest crewWild

Crews operate combines from Texas to the Canadian border every summer, chasing the wheat harvest north. Long days, real machinery, and a slice of America most college graduates will never see from the inside.

55Get on a nonprofit boardNetwork

Most boards are short on young people who can raise money and manage volunteers, and they rarely advertise it. If you want something, ask nicely for it.

56Become a Coding it Forward fellowPublic

Ten weeks placing early-career technologists inside federal, state, and local government offices across engineering, data, design, and product. All majors welcome with demonstrated ability, and a 700-plus alumni network in civic tech.

57Join the Urban Leaders FellowshipPublic

Seven weeks working alongside elected officials across nine cities, including Kansas City, with a stipend and several application deadlines. Low commitment, high access.

58Win an Obama-Chesky Voyager ScholarshipSchool

Up to $50,000 in last-dollar aid, a funded Summer Voyage you design yourself between junior and senior year, and lifetime access to the Obama Foundation network. Four hundred students have received it since 2022. Read the eligibility carefully: you apply as a sophomore heading into junior year, and you must demonstrate financial need. March deadline.

59Become a Kleiner Perkins FellowFrontier

A paid summer inside a top-tier startup, with an alumni network that includes the founders of Figma, Rippling, and Robinhood. Engineering, product, and design leaning. Applications open in the fall and close January 31.

60Staff a congressional officePublic

Two years on Capitol Hill buys a permanent Washington network at an age when nobody expects you to have one. Entry-level staff assistant roles turn over constantly.

61Work in a mayor’s officePublic

City government moves faster than federal, and at the end you can point at a street, a park, or a program that changed because you were there.

62Staff a state legislaturePublic

Low barrier to entry, high access, and you learn how the rules that govern every business you’ll ever operate get written.

63Work for an economic development organizationPublic

You’ll meet nearly every business owner in your region inside a year. For a future acquirer or founder, that contact list is the asset.

64Become an associate at a search fundOwn

Searchers raise capital specifically to find and buy one company. Working alongside one, you see deal sourcing, diligence, financing, and the handoff into the operator seat, which is the exact sequence you’ll lead yourself later.

65Take the Alaska Fellows ProgramFellowship

Fall to spring, residential, placed with nonprofits and public-sector organizations in Anchorage, Juneau, Sitka, or Fairbanks, with housing, a stipend, and communal living. Unusual enough that people will ask you about it for twenty years.

66Get a Boren Award or Critical Language ScholarshipGlobal

Federal funding to learn a strategically important language: Arabic, Mandarin, Russian, Swahili, Korean, and others. Boren carries a federal service requirement, which is a feature rather than a bug if you want a government career.

67Apply for a Pickering or Rangel FellowshipPublic

Both fund graduate school and lead straight into the U.S. Foreign Service. If diplomacy interests you at all, these are the two cleanest doors into it, and hardly anyone knows they exist.

68Become a Kiva FellowGlobal

Field placements with microfinance institutions in developing economies. You see credit, repayment, and small business economics in the hardest conditions there are, and it changes how you read a balance sheet for good.

69Serve a season with the Student Conservation Association or a state conservation corpsWild

Trail crews, park operations, and field science support across the national park system. Cheap to enter, physically demanding, and it puts you in landscapes most people only visit.

70Deploy with Team RubiconWild

Disaster response built around veterans and civilians, deploying to hurricanes, floods, and tornadoes. Training is free and deployments last days to weeks, so you can do it around a job. Working inside an incident command structure is a real management education.

71Get commissioned as a military officerWild

Officer Candidate School and direct commission routes exist for graduates. You’ll lead people in their late teens and early twenties under conditions no civilian job replicates, and the veteran network is one of the most durable in American business.

72Become an EMT or a volunteer firefighterCraft

A few months of night classes. You learn to decide with incomplete information while your heart rate is at 160, which is a skill no MBA program teaches.

73Earn a private pilot’s licenseCraft

Forty to sixty hours of flight time. It’s a rigorous, checklist-driven discipline that changes how you think about preparation, and it opens conversations in rooms you haven’t entered yet.

74Get a commercial driver’s licenseCraft

Two to seven weeks. If you ever buy a services business, manage logistics, or need to move equipment yourself at 2 a.m. before a big job, you’ll be glad you did.

75Go to auction schoolCraft

Two weeks, and you come out able to lead a live auction. It’s a rare, memorable credential, it sharpens public speaking and crowd reading fast, and it pays off if you ever operate in real estate, equipment, estates, or fundraising.

76Become elite at enterprise salesCraft

Not comfortable with it. Elite. Selling in a driveway taught you courage. Enterprise selling teaches you to navigate five stakeholders across a twelve-month cycle. It’s the only skill that is a job, a multiplier on every other job, and a permanent hedge against unemployment all at once.

77Ship a product that strangers pay forCraft

Not a class project and not a pitch deck. Something with a checkout button and revenue from people you’ve never met. The gap between people who build and people who talk is widening fast, and AI tools mean you no longer need a computer science degree to close it.

78Work at a quantitative trading firmFrontier

Firms like Jane Street, Citadel Securities, and Optiver hire new graduates on raw reasoning ability rather than pedigree, pay extremely well, and select through probability and game-theory interviews you can prepare for.

79Do two years as an investment banking analyst, then leaveCraft

Brutal hours and a real education in how deals, valuation, and financing work. The mistake is staying a third year out of inertia. Take the training and go operate.

80Join a private equity operating teamCraft

Not the investment side, the operating side, where people get dropped into portfolio companies to fix things. It’s applied general management with a scoreboard, and it’s the skill set you’ve been building all along.

81Become an ALDI District ManagerOperate

The closest external analog to what you already do. You manage store compliance, payroll budgets, loss results, inventory audits, hiring, and direct reports across a district. Estimated total pay lands in the mid-eighties on average, and they operate a dedicated internship pipeline into the role. Go in clear-eyed: employees describe extensive training and six-figure money alongside real strain on work-life balance.

82Enter a trainee or rotational leadership programOperate

Enterprise, Sherwin-Williams, Cintas, and Fastenal anchor the classic operator circuit, and most Fortune 500s offer one to three year rotations through business functions ending in a permanent placement. You trade autonomy for brand recognition, structured mentorship, and a peer cohort that scatters across the company. Hiring managers at these firms understand what your season means.

83Work at an Inc. 5000 or Inc. Regionals honoreeOperate

Inc. ranks the fastest-growing private companies in America by revenue growth. The Midwest regional list covers Kansas, Missouri, Illinois, Iowa, Michigan, Minnesota, and more. Pull the list, filter by your city, and email the founders directly. Past honorees include Microsoft, Patagonia, and Chobani, long before anyone knew the names.

84Roll up two or three local service businessesOwn

Buy one home improvement company. Then buy the one across town. Consolidation in fragmented local services is where an enormous amount of quiet American wealth gets built, and almost nobody your age is trying it.

85Start a recruiting or staffing firmOwn

You spent a season finding, screening, motivating, and retaining young workers under time pressure. That’s the entire product, and you already know whether you’re good at it.

86Write publicly every week for a yearNetwork

Fifty-two posts about what you’re learning as an operator. It compounds into a reputation, then a network, then inbound opportunity. Most people quit at post nine, which is exactly why finishing works.

87Start a podcast and interview people you want to knowNetwork

The best excuse ever invented for getting thirty minutes with someone who would never answer your cold email.

88Speak at a conference in your industryNetwork

Regional and trade conferences are always short on speakers with real operating stories. Submit a talk. The stage turns you from attendee into authority in one afternoon.

89Move to a city where you know nobodyWild

You built a customer base from zero once. Do it again with a social life, and you’ll never again fear a relocation, a transfer, or a new market.

90Thru-hike a long trailWild

Four to six months on the Appalachian, Pacific Crest, or Continental Divide Trail. Roughly one in four who start the AT finish it. Employers read completion as exactly what it is.

91Take a NOLS or Outward Bound instructor courseWild

Wilderness leadership training that certifies you to lead groups in serious terrain. Leadership development with the conference rooms and slide decks stripped out.

92Learn financial modeling and read statements coldCraft

Non-negotiable if you intend to buy a business, raise capital, or manage a P&L above $5 million. Most operators fake this for a decade, and it costs them at the negotiating table.

93Compete in Toastmasters through the district levelCraft

The path toward the World Championship of Public Speaking is a structured, judged, repeatable way to get excellent on a stage. Cheap, weekly, and the feedback is merciless in the best way.

94Join or start a mastermind that meets monthly for a yearNetwork

Five to eight peers, real numbers on the table, no performing. The people who tell you the truth on a bad month are worth more than any conference you’ll ever attend.

95Find a mentor fifteen years ahead of you and close the loopNetwork

Ask for advice, act on it, and report back with results. Almost nobody does the third part, which is the whole reason mentors stop returning calls.

96Coach a first-year intern through a full seasonOperate

Nothing sharpens your own operating philosophy like owning someone else’s outcome. In twenty years your reputation will be built out of the people you developed, not the revenue you booked.

97Recruit a co-founder out of your own branch networkNetwork

Co-founder conflict kills more young companies than the market does. You’ve already watched this person handle a bad week, a missed number, and an angry customer, which is more due diligence than most founders ever do.

98Get a graduate degree, but be ruthless about which oneSchool

Three rules. First, if it’s an MBA, make it Harvard, Stanford, Wharton, Booth, Kellogg, MIT Sloan, or Columbia. Below that tier the network and the recruiting pipeline thin out fast, and you’re paying six figures plus two years of forgone income for a credential the market quietly discounts. Second, wait five years. The best programs want operators, not resume collectors, and you’ll get far more out of the room at 27 than at 22. Third, if you want a specific capability rather than a general credential, take a one-year specialized master’s in finance, analytics, or computer science instead. Faster, cheaper, more useful than a mid-tier MBA. Don’t get a degree just to have one.

99Apply to Kauffman Fellows once you’re already in ventureSchool

The most prestigious program in venture capital, and a two-year program for people already working in the industry, with tuition around $80,000 and recent classes skewing toward partner-level investors. It’s an accelerant, not an entry point. Get the venture job first.

100Operate a weekly loop against a ten-year goalCraft

Commit, act, measure, adjust, then do it again. Write the ten-year goal down, work backward to three years, one year, and ninety days, and review it every week with someone who will ask. Motivation fades. A loop does not. Every item above works better attached to one.

What to do this week

You can’t go after all 100. Pick three, put a date on each, and tell someone who will ask you about it.

If you want the fastest start, a few doors close soon. The One Young World Leading Scholarship closes October 31. The Congress-Bundestag Youth Exchange deadline falls around November 1. Emergent Ventures is rolling and takes thirty minutes, so there’s no reason not to have applied by Friday.

If Schwarzman or Fulbright caught your eye, this year’s window has closed. Put next year’s deadline on your calendar now and start the application in the spring, when everyone else is still thinking about it.

Pick three, then commit to the loop

A goal without a loop is a wish. Put yours on one.
